\begin{abstract}
\noindent The ``abundance'' diagnosis holds that housing markets in productive U.S.\
metros are out of equilibrium because land-use regulation throttles supply
\citep{kleinthompson,hsieh2019housing,glaeser2005}. What that literature largely takes
as exogenous---the stringency of regulation---is the equilibrium output of a \emph{market
for regulation} run through local bodies. This note builds a deliberately small model with
two changes relative to the supply-elasticity tradition. First, regulation operates on
\emph{two margins}: a \emph{by-right envelope} set by the zoning map (what may be built
ministerially, with certainty and no hearing) and a \emph{discretionary friction} applied
to everything that exceeds it (conditional-use authorization, discretionary review,
conditions, and delay). The empirical fact that motivates this split is that approvals,
\emph{conditional on reaching a vote}, are nearly universal: restriction does not operate
through denial at the hearing but through what must be endured to reach it. Second, the
metro is \emph{fragmented}: one integrated labor market and a handful of housing
submarkets are regulated by $\sim$100 jurisdictions whose boundaries align with neither, so
each jurisdiction's choice of both margins is the solution to a game played against its
neighbors \citep{brueckner1998testing,fischel2001homevoter}. Every object maps to a field
in an item-level dataset built from planning-commission minutes (1998--present) under a
single canonical schema. The point is measurability: a model whose primitives are the
things the minutes record, and---crucially---a model that locates restriction where the
record shows it actually lives.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and relation to the literature}

A large body of work establishes that the post-1970 divergence between house prices and
construction costs in high-demand metros is a supply phenomenon driven by land-use
regulation \citep{glaeser2005,glaeser2005empirical,glaeser2009causes,quigley2005effects},
and that the resulting spatial misallocation is macroeconomically large
\citep{hsieh2019housing}. Measurement has advanced from survey indices
\citep{gyourko2008new,gyourko2021local,gyourko2015regulation} to LLM-based generative
regulatory measurement \citep{bartik2024costs}. Yet both approaches measure regulatory
\emph{outcomes}; neither observes the \emph{process} that produces them.

The political-economy ingredients are well known in isolation.
\citet{fischel2004economic} formalizes the ``homevoter''; \citet{ortalo2014political} study
homeownership versus affordability in a voting model; \citet{jones2022partisanship}
document how partisanship shapes housing policy; \citet{weiss1988real} traces the
real-estate industry's hand in San Francisco's original zoning regime.

\paragraph{What is new here.} Three features the existing work abstracts away are exactly
the features the administrative record makes visible. (i) Regulation is \emph{two
instruments, not one level}: the map sets a by-right envelope, and a separate discretionary
process taxes whatever exceeds it. (ii) That discretionary tax operates through
\emph{process friction}---the requirement of a hearing, the mobilization of opposition,
conditions, and delay---rather than through denial, which is rare. (iii) The metro is
\emph{fragmented}, so both instruments are chosen jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction in a game
coupled through shared prices and a shared labor market, making spillovers and strategic
interaction first-class objects \citep{brueckner1998testing,brueckner2003strategic}. The
contribution is a tractable equilibrium object with all three features, stated in terms a
researcher can measure item-by-item across many jurisdictions.

\section{Environment}\label{sec:env}

One productive metro---the Bay Area---comprises $J$ jurisdictions $j=1,\dots,J$ and a
competitive ``rest of country'' delivering reservation utility $\bar u$. The defining
feature is a mismatch of boundaries: \emph{one labor market} (all jurisdictions draw on a
common worker pool; a worker earns the regional wage $w_m=w(N)$, $w'>0$, $N\equiv\sum_j
N_j$, capturing Hsieh--Moretti agglomeration rents), \emph{a few housing submarkets}
($s(j)\in\{1,\dots,S\}$, $S\ll J$, within which housing is a close substitute), and
\emph{many regulators} (each $j$ sets its own regulation).

\paragraph{Preferences and prices.} A household of type $\theta$ in $j$ has utility
$U = c + \alpha\ln h - \gamma g(D_j)$, $g'>0$, with $-\gamma g(D_j)$ the local density
disamenity (so low density is a normal good and high-$\theta$ residents are the natural
demanders of restriction), and income $y(\theta)=w_m\theta$ regardless of residence---the
labor-market coupling. Within submarket $s$, clearing gives the price index
\begin{equation}
p_{s} = \frac{\alpha N_{s}}{H_{s}}, \qquad
N_s=\!\!\sum_{j:s(j)=s}\!\!N_j,\ \ H_s=\!\!\sum_{j:s(j)=s}\!\!H_j,
\label{eq:price}
\end{equation}
so a unit built in any jurisdiction of $s$ lowers the price faced by incumbents in every
jurisdiction of $s$; density $D_j=N_j/\bar L_j$ is local. The cost of new housing is local;
the benefit (lower submarket price, more regional employment hence higher $w_m$) is shared.

\section{Regulation on two margins}\label{sec:tech}

This section replaces the single ``approval rate'' of earlier drafts with the structure the
record actually exhibits. The motivating fact, visible in the minutes: among items that
reach a commission vote, approval (or its discretionary-review equivalent, declining to
intervene) is nearly universal. Denial is not the instrument. Restriction operates
\emph{before and around} the vote, on two margins.

\paragraph{The by-right envelope.} The zoning map assigns each parcel in $j$ a use district
and a height-and-bulk district, which together define a \emph{by-right envelope}
$\bar z_j$: the scale and use a project may obtain \emph{ministerially}---built with
certainty, no hearing, no discretionary cost. A project with proposed scale/use
$z\le\bar z_j$ clears by right and never appears on the commission calendar. This is why the
commission's \emph{conditional} approval rate is uninformative about stringency: the
by-right projects, the bulk of conforming construction, are invisible to the hearing record
by construction.

\paragraph{The discretionary tax.} A project that requires more than the envelope
allows---a use not principally permitted, a scale above the limit, a parcel in a special
district---must enter discretionary review (conditional-use authorization, variance,
mandatory or requested discretionary review). It is built only after enduring a
\emph{discretionary tax} $\tau_j$ whose components are exactly what the minutes record. Each
component below names the schema field(s) that identify it and how it is obtained---directly
labeled, derived post-hoc, or (for two components) deferred pending a second source:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Process exposure}---the requirement of a hearing at all, i.e.\ the share of
activity the envelope forces off the by-right path. \emph{Labeled:} the
\texttt{request\_type} enum (conditional\_use, discretionary\_review, variance, \dots) and
\texttt{special\_use\_district}.
\item \textbf{Mobilized opposition}---the disamenity $g(D_j)$ made politically operative.
\emph{Labeled (modern era $\approx$2015+):}
\texttt{oppose\_count}/\texttt{support\_count}/\texttt{neutral\_count} (stance markers
$+/-/=$) and the grounds in \texttt{speaker\_statements}.
\item \textbf{Disposition and conditioning}---whether the commission approved, declined to
intervene, conditioned, or denied. \emph{Labeled:} the \texttt{action} enum, which for
discretionary-review items uses the dedicated values \texttt{did\_not\_take\_dr} /
\texttt{took\_dr} / \texttt{took\_dr\_and\_approved}; the \emph{margin} \texttt{vote} is
\emph{derived} in coercion from $|\texttt{ayes}|-|\texttt{noes}|$. The conditioning
\emph{content} (\texttt{modifications} free text) is captured where present but is sparse;
treat conditioning as an indicator (\texttt{action}$=$approved\_with\_conditions /
approved\_as\_modified) rather than a measured intensity.
\item \textbf{Delay}---continuances, which raise carrying cost and induce attrition.
\emph{Derived (post-hoc):} \texttt{action}$=$continued plus a case-level rollup---hearings
per case and first-to-final-disposition span---built by joining item records on
\texttt{case\_number} across meetings. The item-level schema does not express this directly;
it is a downstream table, not a labeled field.
\item \textbf{Denial}---rare, but present. \emph{Labeled:} \texttt{action}$=$disapproved or
\texttt{took\_dr} against the project.
\end{enumerate}
Two finer measures of the tax---the project's scale \emph{above} the by-right envelope, and
the affordable/exaction concessions extracted---are \emph{not} captured in this build:
proposed stories/height are deferred to a derived pass over \texttt{project\_descr}, and
inclusionary terms live in staff reports outside the minutes corpus. The model therefore
identifies $\tau_j$ from process exposure, opposition, disposition/conditioning, delay, and
denial, and treats the scale-above-envelope and exaction margins as extensions contingent on
those additional sources.

\begin{definition}[Effective regulation as a composite of two margins]
Effective stringency in $j$ is $r_j=g(\bar z_j,\tau_j)$, increasing in the tightness of the
by-right envelope (smaller $\bar z_j$ pushes more activity into the discretionary channel)
and in the discretionary tax $\tau_j$ (heavier process exposure, opposition, conditioning,
and delay). Crucially, $r_j$ can rise with \emph{no change in the conditional approval
rate}: a jurisdiction restricts by shrinking $\bar z_j$ or raising $\tau_j$, not by denying
more of what reaches a vote.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{The developer's problem.} A candidate project of latent scale/use $z$ chooses how
far to push above the envelope. Surplus from scale $z$ is $\Pi(z)$, increasing and concave;
the discretionary cost of exceeding the envelope is $\tau_j\cdot\max\{z-\bar z_j,0\}$.
The developer builds $z^\ast_j=\arg\max_z \Pi(z)-\tau_j\max\{z-\bar z_j,0\}$, files at
$z^\ast_j$ if the net surplus is positive, and otherwise withdraws (or never files). Three
empirical signatures follow: (i) bunching of projects at the envelope $\bar z_j$ (testable
once proposed scale is recovered from \texttt{project\_descr}); (ii) near-universal approval
\emph{conditional on filing above the envelope}, because projects that would be denied are
deterred or shaved first---directly visible in the \texttt{action} distribution; (iii)
restriction showing up as withdrawals, continuances, conditions, and a thin above-envelope
calendar, not as denials---visible in \texttt{action} and the case-level continuance rollup.

\begin{definition}[New supply]
New construction in $j$ aggregates by-right building (projects with $z\le\bar z_j$, built
with certainty) and surviving above-envelope projects:
$\Delta H_j=\kappa_j^{\text{BR}}+\int \mathbf{1}\{\Pi(z^\ast_j)-\tau_j(z^\ast_j-\bar z_j)\ge
0\}\,z^\ast_j\,dF(z)$. The discretionary record observes only the second term; the first is
recovered from the map and permit data.
\end{definition}

\section{The political mechanism: homevoters choose \texorpdfstring{$(\bar z_j,\tau_j)$}{(z\_j, tau\_j)}}\label{sec:pol}

Following the homevoter logic \citep{fischel2004economic}, jurisdiction $j$'s authorities
are vote-maximizers answering to incumbent residents, a share $\lambda_j$ of whom are
homeowners holding local housing as their principal asset; $\phi_j$ is the political weight
on incumbents. The two margins map naturally to two bodies: the \emph{city council} sets the
map $\bar z_j$ (legislative, infrequent), and the \emph{planning commission} sets the
discretionary tax $\tau_j$ through its revealed disposition behavior (case-by-case,
high-frequency). This resolves the ``unitary authority'' shortcut of earlier drafts: the map
is a slow state variable, the discretionary tax a fast one.

An incumbent homeowner's payoff from her jurisdiction's regulation, taking neighbors'
policies as given, is
\begin{equation}
W^{\text{own}}_j = \underbrace{p_{s(j)}\big(\bar z_{-j},\tau_{-j};\bar z_j,\tau_j\big)}_{\text{submarket asset value}}
 \;-\; \underbrace{\gamma\,g\!\big(D_j(\bar z_j,\tau_j)\big)}_{\text{local density disamenity}} ,
\label{eq:owner}
\end{equation}
where both $\bar z_j$ and $\tau_j$ raise the asset value (by restricting local supply) and
reduce the disamenity, while only \emph{regional} supply matters for the price an incumbent's
asset commands. Renters/entrants (weight $1-\phi_j\lambda_j$) prefer abundant regional
supply. The authority chooses $(\bar z_j,\tau_j)$ to maximize
$\Omega_j=\phi_j\lambda_j W^{\text{own}}_j+(1-\phi_j\lambda_j)W^{\text{ent}}_j$.

\paragraph{The fragmentation wedge.} As in the single-margin model, the effect of $j$'s own
supply on its submarket price is attenuated by submarket size: $\partial p_{s(j)}/\partial
(\text{supply}_j)=-(p_s/H_s)$. A jurisdiction small within its submarket bears the full local
disamenity of any approval but captures little of the price relief (it leaks to
co-submarket neighbors), so it restricts on \emph{both} margins.

\begin{proposition}[Substitution between the two margins under preemption]\label{prop:sub}
Suppose state preemption (e.g.\ ministerial-approval mandates, by-right upzoning, a
builder's remedy) raises the marginal cost to $j$ of a tight envelope---forcing $\bar z_j$
up or converting discretionary categories to ministerial ones---while leaving the marginal
cost of discretionary friction $\tau_j$ unchanged. Then a jurisdiction whose homevoter bloc
still demands restriction substitutes \emph{from the envelope to the tax}: $\bar z_j$ rises
(the map loosens, as the mandate intends) while $\tau_j$ rises in compensation (more items
pushed to conditions, more opposition entertained, more continuances). Effective stringency
$r_j=g(\bar z_j,\tau_j)$ falls by less than the change in $\bar z_j$ alone would imply, and
\emph{the conditional approval rate does not move}. A scalar index, or an approval-rate
measure, registers the reform as working; the decomposed record reveals the offset.
\end{proposition}

\noindent This is the central testable mechanism and the reason both data margins are
required. It is invisible to any one-dimensional measure of stringency.

\begin{proposition}[Fragmentation intensifies restriction on both margins]\label{prop:frag}
Holding $(\phi_j\lambda_j,\gamma,\bar L_j)$ fixed, a jurisdiction's restriction on each
margin is increasing in the number of jurisdictions sharing its housing submarket. As $j$
becomes atomistic within $s$, the price-relief motive vanishes, the local-disamenity motive
dominates, and both $\bar z_j$ tightens and $\tau_j$ rises toward their restrictive corners.
\end{proposition}

\section{Strategic interaction and regional equilibrium}\label{sec:eq}

Because $p_{s(j)}$ depends on neighbors' regulation, \eqref{eq:owner} defines a game with
reaction functions on each margin.

\begin{proposition}[Regulation is strategically substitutable within a submarket]\label{prop:react}
For co-submarket jurisdictions, $\partial R_j/\partial(\text{loosening}_k)<0$: if a neighbor
loosens (raises $\bar z_k$ or lowers $\tau_k$), the submarket price falls, raising a
homevoter-pivotal $j$'s incentive to restrict. Each jurisdiction free-rides on others'
supply; the Nash equilibrium has less regional construction than the submarket-coordinated
optimum.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Regional over-restriction and demand-shock amplification]\label{prop:welfare}
A metro planner internalizing entrants' access to the agglomeration rent and the
cross-jurisdiction price externality chooses looser regulation on both margins for every
$j$ where the homevoter bloc is pivotal; the wedge grows in $\phi_j\lambda_j$, in the
agglomeration rent, and in submarket fragmentation. A positive regional demand shock raises
every $p_s$, raising the asset-value return to restriction, so each jurisdiction tightens;
strategic substitutability (Prop.~\ref{prop:react}) propagates the tightening across
co-submarket neighbors. The regional price response exceeds both the fixed-regulation and
the single-jurisdiction response.
\end{proposition}

\section{Extensions}\label{sec:ext}

\paragraph{Credit / CRA.} An easing raises the demand shifter $\alpha\to\alpha(1+\mu)$
\citep{favara2015credit,di2017credit}, raising $p_s$ and (Prop.~\ref{prop:welfare})
tightening regulation, so part of a credit boom shows up as tighter discretion rather than
more building. The CRA's tract-level eligibility generates within-metro, within-submarket
variation in $\mu$ \citep{saadi2020role,brevoort2024reexamining}; the minutes locate each
project (address $\to$ tract $\to$ jurisdiction $\to$ submarket), so the relevant object is
the interaction of tract credit shocks with the local discretionary response.

\paragraph{Dynamics: ratchet and contagion.} Let the homeowner share evolve with prices,
$\lambda_{j,t+1}=\lambda_{j,t}+\eta(p_{s(j),t}-\bar p)$. With submarket price linkage and
strategic substitutability, a shock that tips one jurisdiction into a restrictive basin
raises the submarket price, entrenching homeowners and tipping co-submarket neighbors: the
restrictive regime is sticky within and \emph{contagious across} jurisdictions. This
motivates the long panel (1998--present, $\geq$ one price cycle) across jurisdictions.

\paragraph{Toward identification.} Reduced-form tests, ordered by how cleanly the current
data supports them:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Staff--commission override (Prop.~\ref{prop:welfare}):} the cleanest, because
it is a \emph{within-item} comparison---regress an override-toward-restriction indicator
(\texttt{preliminary\_recommendation} more permissive than \texttt{action}) on the project
tract's homeownership share and on \texttt{oppose\_count}. The override indicator is
\emph{derived} by normalizing \texttt{preliminary\_recommendation} onto the \texttt{action}
enum---a post-processing step, not a relabeling cost.
\item \textbf{Margin substitution under preemption (Prop.~\ref{prop:sub}):} difference-in-differences
around SB-9/35/423 / builder's-remedy exposure, with the \emph{envelope} (map) and the
\emph{discretionary tax} (minutes) as separate outcomes; predict $\bar z$ loosens while
$\tau$ rises and the conditional approval rate is flat.
\item \textbf{Strategic interaction (Prop.~\ref{prop:react}):} spatial-lag reaction function
of $j$'s discretionary tax on co-submarket neighbors' tax, instrumented by neighbors'
exogenous characteristics \citep{brueckner1998testing,brueckner2003strategic}. Needs the
multi-jurisdiction build.
\item \textbf{Feedback / amplification (Prop.~\ref{prop:welfare}):} regress discretionary
friction (opposition, continuances, conditioning) on lagged submarket price growth,
instrumenting price with regional demand or rate shocks.
\item \textbf{Mobilization $\to$ disamenity:} code \texttt{speaker\_statements} into grounds
(shadow, traffic, character, precedent) to test whether opposition intensity tracks the
density terms the model assigns to $g(D_j)$.
\end{itemize}

\section{What the build captures, derives, and defers}\label{sec:datafix}

The schema (now 36 fields) and its coercion settle which model objects are labeled directly,
which are derived downstream, and which are deferred to a second source. This division
determines which tests run on this build.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep3pt
\item \textbf{Labeled directly.} Disposition (\texttt{action}, including the dedicated DR
outcomes \texttt{did\_not\_take\_dr}/\texttt{took\_dr}/\texttt{took\_dr\_and\_approved}),
process exposure (\texttt{request\_type}), opposition (\texttt{oppose\_/support\_/neutral\_count},
modern era), the roll-call (\texttt{ayes}/\texttt{noes}/\texttt{recused}), zoning districts,
the assigned planner (\texttt{staff\_planner}), and the spatial/time keys. These support the
override, fragmentation, strategic-interaction, and amplification tests directly.
\item \textbf{Derived, not labeled.} The vote margin \texttt{vote} is computed in coercion
from $|\texttt{ayes}|-|\texttt{noes}|$ (never overwriting a stated tally). The
staff--commission override indicator is a post-processing comparison of
\texttt{preliminary\_recommendation} (normalized onto the \texttt{action} enum) with
\texttt{action}. The delay margin---hearings per case and first-to-final-disposition
span---is a case-level rollup joining item records on \texttt{case\_number}. None consume
labeling surface.
\item \textbf{Deferred to additional sources or passes.} The project's scale \emph{above}
the by-right envelope (proposed stories/height) is recoverable from \texttt{project\_descr}
but is left to a validated derived pass, because construction blocks carry multiple
story figures (existing, addition, total) that a naive extractor confuses; until that pass is
validated, the scale-bunching signature is untested. The discretionary \emph{trigger} (which
envelope margin binds) requires a code-section$\to$category lookup and is deferred to a
post-hoc NLP pass emitting a list. Affordable/inclusionary terms (the exaction margin) live
in staff reports outside the minutes and are deferred pending that corpus. Final disposition
after appeal lives in the Board of Supervisors / Board of Appeals series and is out of scope
for a minutes-only build.
\end{itemize}
The practical consequence for the model: the discretionary tax $\tau_j$ is identified in this
build from process exposure, opposition, disposition/conditioning, derived delay, and
denial. The scale-above-envelope and exaction refinements (\S\ref{sec:tech}) are extensions
that switch on when the deferred sources are added---they sharpen $\tau_j$ but are not
required for the central two-margin substitution test (Prop.~\ref{prop:sub}), which needs
only the map (envelope) and the discretionary disposition record (tax).

\section{Conclusion}

Two moves---splitting regulation into a by-right envelope and a discretionary tax that
operates through process friction rather than denial, and embedding many such jurisdictions
in a game coupled through shared prices and a shared labor market---deliver a model whose
restriction lives where the minutes show it lives, whose central prediction (substitution
between the two margins under preemption, Prop.~\ref{prop:sub}) requires exactly the two
data dimensions the project builds, and whose strategic and dynamic results
(Props.~\ref{prop:react}--\ref{prop:welfare}) motivate the multi-jurisdiction, long-panel
extension. The near-universal conditional approval rate, far from a nuisance, is the
equilibrium fingerprint of a regime that restricts before and around the vote rather than
at it.
